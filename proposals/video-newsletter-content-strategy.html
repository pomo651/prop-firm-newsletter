Short + newsletter: how many stories?

Two content proposals for Funded Tape, plus a recommendation

PREPARED NOV 3, 2026  ·  FOR POMO651  ·  INTERNAL, NOT PUBLISHED
✅ RECOMMENDATION

Go with Proposal B, with a cap: Top 5 + "More this week" (3 to 6 verified quick hits) + a small rule-change tracker.

  • It gives video viewers a reason to click. If the newsletter only repeats the Short's 5 stories, "more depth" is a weak promise. "5 more stories + every rule change" is a concrete one.
  • Length is not the risk people think. A 100,000-email study found no clear link between word count and click rate. The real cost is a slight rise in unsubscribes on longer emails, so keep quick hits to 1 to 2 lines and cap the count.
  • It's a proven roundup shape. TLDR and Morning Brew both pair a few main stories with a fast "quick links" section.
  • Hold "Deals & promo codes" for now. Discount codes change daily and are usually tied to affiliate money, which is what this week's Prop Firm Match story was about. Add it later only with clear affiliate labels.
💡 One thing to fix first: links in a Short's description and comments aren't clickable. The Short's CTA should point to the newsletter link on our YouTube channel page, which is clickable. A per-Short "Related video" link is clickable too, but it can only point to our own YouTube videos.

🎯 The question

Once there's a weekly Short, viewers already get the gist of the Top 5 in about 90 seconds. Should the newsletter carry more than 5 items, so the video can send people to it "for more"?

SCHEDULE (FIXED)Collection runs on a rolling 7-day window, with the news cutoff Sat 5 PM ET (3 PM Edmonton). The draft is ready Sat 8 PM ET (6 PM), you approve by 10 PM ET (8 PM), and it sends Sun 9 AM ET (7 AM).
RULE (FIXED)Every claim is checked against a primary source and linked. If it can't be verified, it's dropped. This week it cut several unconfirmed claims and items.
TEAMFeed (official sources), Pulse (community), Editor (writes), Reel (the Short), and KFC (reviews, brings to you). We're on Beehiiv's free plan, which has no A/B testing.

🔍 What the evidence says

YOUTUBE SHORTS LINKS (VERIFIED ON YOUTUBE HELP)
  • "URLs placed in YouTube Shorts comments and Shorts descriptions are non-clickable." That applies to pinned comments too.
  • Clickable: channel profile links (up to 14) and the Related video link in the Shorts player.
  • Related video must be your own public or unlisted video, and needs advanced feature access.
  • Clickable links in long-form video descriptions also need advanced features.
YouTube Help: Sharing links · Add a related video to Shorts · Feature access
EMAIL LENGTH VS ENGAGEMENT (MIXED)
  • Buttondown sampled 100,000 sends and found "no pretty linear relationship" between word count and click rate.
  • Inntopia (1,200 hotel campaigns) found no clear click-rate link, but opt-outs rose from 0.18% (1 to 100 words) to 0.22% (400+ words).
  • One MarketingExperiments test found shorter copy got a 16.9% higher clickthrough. It was a single promotional email, so treat it as a weak signal.
Buttondown · Inntopia · MarketingExperiments
HOW BIG ROUNDUPS ARE BUILT
  • TLDR (per a 21-issue review): about 14 linked items in a fixed order, ending in "Quick Links" with five or six one-line items. A 1,340-word email "reads in five minutes."
  • Morning Brew's editor describes a greeting, market snapshot, the day's news, a "quick links roundup section," tips, and a puzzle.
Hark: TLDR review · Morning Brew: Neal Freyman interview
WHAT TO MEASURE
  • Open rates are inflated by machine opens (Apple Mail Privacy Protection), so judge changes on clicks, replies and unsubscribes.
  • I found no public data on how often Shorts viewers click through to a newsletter. Expect a small share, and treat the size of this effect as a judgment call.
Distinctful on open-rate noise

🅿️ Proposal A: "Same Top 5, two formats"

SHORT = TEASER · NEWSLETTER = DEPTH
What subscribers getThe same 5 stories as the Short, with full write-ups: the numbers, the 💡 Why it matters box, and every source link. That's roughly this week's edition (about 900 words).
Reason to click from the video"Full details, numbers and sources." It's honest, but viewers who just heard the 5 stories may feel they already got it.
Opens, reads, growthThe short email keeps read-through high and unsubscribes low (Inntopia's opt-out trend). For growth, though, the Short and the email overlap almost fully, so the video does less to convert viewers into subscribers. That last point is a judgment call.
Bot workloadUnchanged. Feed and Pulse shortlist, Editor writes 5 stories, Reel makes the Short from the same 5, and KFC reviews one set. This is the lowest load.
Fact-check riskLowest: only 5 items to check.
Fits Sat cutoff / Sun 9 AM ETYes, with room to spare. The 3-hour gap from cutoff to draft is plenty.
Pros
  • Simplest and fastest to run
  • One story list for both formats
  • Lowest error risk
Cons
  • Weak reason to click from the video
  • Verified items that miss the Top 5 are wasted
  • Hard to stand out from free YouTube recaps

🅱️ Proposal B: "Short = Top 5, newsletter = Top 5 + more"

SHORT = TOP 5 · NEWSLETTER = TOP 5 + MORE
What subscribers getThe same Top 5 with full write-ups, then "More this week": 3 to 6 quick hits of 1 to 2 lines, each with one primary-source link. Then a small rule-change tracker (firm, what changed, which accounts it applies to, link). Adds roughly 200 to 350 words.
Reason to click from the videoConcrete and new: "5 more stories + every rule change this week, in the newsletter." Viewers get something the video didn't give them.
Opens, reads, growthOpens are driven by the subject line and sender, not by length, so expect little change. Clicks per reader should rise because there are more links to tap (Inntopia saw clicks per clicker climb with length). Unsubscribes may tick up slightly on a longer email, so keep the cap. Growth should improve from the stronger video CTA, but there's no public benchmark, so that's a judgment call to test.
Bot workloadFeed and Pulse: they already see far more items than make the Top 5, so the change is tagging each with a primary link as they find it. Editor: about 30 to 45 extra minutes for the 1-line write-ups and the tracker (my estimate). KFC: a longer review. Reel: only the end card and pinned comment text change.
Fact-check riskHigher: 8 to 11 items instead of 5. Handle it with the same rule (primary source or drop). Verify quick hits during the week as they come in, not on Saturday night. If too few clear the bar, the section shrinks; it never gets padded.
Fits Sat cutoff / Sun 9 AM ETYes, if quick hits are verified on a rolling basis Monday to Saturday. The Sat 5 PM ET cutoff, 8 PM ET draft and Sun 9 AM ET send don't change. Your 2-hour approval stays realistic because quick hits read in about a minute.
Pros
  • Clear reason to subscribe from the video
  • Uses verified items that miss the Top 5
  • The rule tracker is a useful weekly reference
  • Matches proven roundup formats
Cons
  • More to verify each week
  • Longer email: slight unsubscribe risk
  • Pressure to fill the section; the cap and the drop rule guard against padding

📊 How we'd check it worked (first 4 editions)

  • Track in Beehiiv each week: click rate, clicks on "More this week" links, unsubscribes, and replies.
  • Use a dedicated signup link for YouTube, such as a tagged URL or its own landing page, so YouTube signups can be told apart.
  • Decision rule: keep B if unsubscribes don't rise noticeably and the quick hits get clicks. Otherwise trim to 3 quick hits, or fall back to A.
  • Before Reel posts: get a newsletter link onto the channel profile (clickable) and check Related video eligibility, which needs advanced features.
